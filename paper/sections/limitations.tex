\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Simplified physics.} The forward model is partial-volume averaging, a Gaussian PSF and
        white Gaussian noise added in the image domain. Real CT noise is correlated by the
        reconstruction filter and is signal dependent, and real volumes contain beam-hardening, ring and
        streak artefacts. A projection-domain simulation (forward projection, Poisson noise, filtered
        back-projection) would be the natural next step.
  \item \textbf{One isolated object per volume.} Porous media and lattices consist of many connected or
        touching structures, and the correction may depend on how much of the surface is close to other
        surfaces. The models have not been tested on such data.
  \item \textbf{Fixed acquisition parameters.} Blur, contrast and the iso-level are fixed, and the models
        are trained at two grid sizes and three noise levels. Their behaviour between and outside these
        settings is untested; in particular, a model trained at $\sigma_n\le0.15$ should not be trusted at
        higher noise.
  \item \textbf{Sub-voxel roughness is not observable.} Surface detail much smaller than a voxel does not
        survive voxelization. A learned model can only add back the excess area it expects from the
        roughness statistics of its training data, so its correction on rough surfaces is a prior, not
        a measurement, and it will be wrong if the real roughness spectrum differs.
  \item \textbf{Limited shape space.} The training shapes are star-shaped or of genus at most one.
        Shapes with many handles, thin sheets or concave pockets are not represented.
  \item \textbf{No tuning, single seed.} Architectures and hyperparameters were fixed in advance, the
        $64^3$ CNN was trained for only 15 epochs because of the compute budget (a single laptop), and
        each model was trained once. Differences of a few hundredths of a percent between models should
        not be over-interpreted.
  \item \textbf{Real data.} No measurement on a real CT scan with a known surface area is included.
\end{itemize}
